# Tarot Source Acquisition Plan

This is Guardian's roadmap for what kinds of Tarot source material to
acquire next, derived from the gap analysis run against the six-layer
model in `synthesis/tarot/README.md` (using The Fool's extraction as the
concrete test case). It defines **capabilities to fill, not books to
buy** — deliberately, so the search for actual sources stays open rather
than anchored to one title. Once a source is acquired, it goes through
the normal `sources/README.md` registration SOP like anything else; this
document doesn't change that pipeline, it just tells us what's worth
scanning for.

## How to read each entry

- **Why it's needed** — what breaks or stays empty without it.
- **Ideal source characteristics** — what to look for, independent of
  title or author.
- **Layer support** — which of the six layers it strengthens, and how.
- **Priority** — how urgently it blocks card authoring generally, not
  just The Fool.

---

## 1. Iconography / Symbolic Language reference

**Why it's needed.** Layer 2 (Symbolic Language) is currently empty for
every card in the library, not just The Fool — none of the four
registered Tarot sources describes what's actually depicted on a card.
They're interpretive, historical, or methodological texts that assume the
reader already knows the imagery. Without this, Layer 2 stays permanently
blank (the alternative — inventing imagery — is explicitly ruled out).

**Ideal source characteristics.**
- Descriptive in its own voice: states what's depicted (figures, objects,
  gestures, setting, color) without immediately jumping to what it means.
  Interpretation-first sources make it hard to separate "what's shown"
  from "what it's said to mean," which defeats the point of a separate
  layer.
- Full-deck coverage, or at least complete Major Arcana coverage as a
  starting point — a source that only illustrates a handful of cards
  reproduces the same unevenness already seen elsewhere in the library.
- Anchored to a specific, identifiable deck tradition. Every other source
  in the library orients around Rider-Waite-Smith (explicitly, in the
  historical/Kabbalah source); an imagery reference for a different deck
  tradition would need to be tracked as a distinct tradition, not blended
  in.
- Consistent per-card structure, so it functions as a reference rather
  than a collection of essays with uneven detail.

**Layer support.** Directly fills Layer 2. Indirectly strengthens Layer 3
(Canonical Meaning) — interpretation grounded in specific, described
symbols is more defensible than interpretation floating free of them —
and Layer 4 (a lens can point at a specific symbol as its evidence,
e.g. "the cliff edge, read psychologically, represents...").

**Priority: HIGH.** Currently a hard blocker for this layer on every
single card, not just unfinished for some of them.

## 2. Baseline / traditional meaning reference

**Why it's needed.** Layer 3 (Canonical Meaning) is meant to be Guardian's
own authored interpretation, *informed by* available traditions — but
there's currently nothing to be informed by for roughly 69 of 78 cards.
The one partial exception (the combinations guide's Wands 1–9 worked
example) covers a single suit. Without a baseline, most cards' Canonical
Meaning would be synthesized from scraps (a phrase from the historical
source, a number-group label) rather than genuinely informed judgment.

**Ideal source characteristics.**
- Full 78-card coverage, stated at real sentence/paragraph depth per
  card — not single keywords, and not a partial worked example.
- States upright and reversed meaning explicitly and separately.
- Consistent structure and voice across the whole deck, so it reads as
  one coherent baseline rather than assembled fragments.
- Shares the same tradition-anchor (Rider-Waite-Smith) as the rest of the
  library, so it's compatible with whatever imagery reference (#1) gets
  acquired — a meaning reference for a different deck tradition would
  need separate handling.
- It does not need to describe imagery itself (that's #1's job) — the two
  gaps are related but should probably be filled by different sources,
  since "describes symbols" and "states traditional meaning" are
  different skills even when a single book happens to do both.

**Layer support.** Directly informs Layer 3 for every card. Also gives
Layer 4 something concrete to depart from — the synthesis format's
"departures from source language" section only means something once
there's a baseline to depart from.

**Priority: HIGH.** Tied with #1 as the most load-bearing gap — every
card's synthesis leans on this layer, and it's currently supported for
about 9 of 78 cards.

## 3. Full-deck psychological / archetypal interpretation

**Why it's needed.** The existing psychological source (used for The
Fool's extraction) has a strong, coherent *framework* — shadow work,
avatar consciousness, feelings vs. emotions, individuation — but applies
it directly to only about 6 of 78 cards. For every other card, applying
this lens means Guardian extending the framework itself, which blurs the
line between "an attributed lens" and "Guardian's own unattributed
addition" — exactly the ambiguity the Fool extraction had to flag
explicitly.

**Ideal source characteristics.**
- Near-full-deck coverage in a genuinely psychological/archetypal voice
  — not historical or esoteric material relabeled as psychological.
- Engages with light/shadow duality per card, which maps directly onto
  the existing schema's light/shadow expression fields.
- A distinct, identifiable authorial voice is fine and expected — this
  is meant to be one explicitly attributed lens among several, not a
  consensus source the way #2 is.

**Layer support.** Deepens Layer 4's psychological lens with real
per-card grounding instead of framework-only coverage. Indirectly useful
for Layer 6 (Guardian Voice) — psychologically grounded material tends to
translate well into reflective, non-prescriptive questions — though
Layer 6 itself always stays Guardian's own writing, never sourced.

**Priority: MEDIUM.** The framework already exists and is usable in a
limited way today; this fills it out rather than building it from
nothing. Less urgent than #1 or #2's near-total gaps.

## 4. Court card reference (structural + interpretive)

**Why it's needed.** No registered source addresses court cards (Page,
Knight, Queen, King across all four suits) at all — neither structurally
(what a rank means, independent of suit) nor interpretively. That's 16 of
78 cards, 20% of the deck, entirely unaddressed by the current library.

**Ideal source characteristics.**
- Explains each rank's meaning as a structural role (what "Knight-ness"
  means across suits), not just per-suit-per-rank in isolation.
- Per-suit court card interpretation, at similar depth to whatever the
  baseline meaning reference (#2) provides for numbered cards.

**Layer support.** Closes a structural gap in Layer 1 and supplies Layer
3 material for a currently-unaddressed fifth of the deck.

**Priority: MEDIUM-LOW, contingent.** Worth checking whether #2, once
acquired, already covers court cards before treating this as a separate
acquisition target — most complete traditional meaning references include
them by default, in which case this item collapses into #2 rather than
requiring something dedicated.

---

## 5. Astrological correspondence source

**Why it's needed.** Layer 4 currently has no real astrological
interpretive lens — only single-word planet/element labels from the
historical/Kabbalah source's numerological grouping, not actual
per-card astrological interpretation.

**Ideal source characteristics.**
- Connects specific planetary/zodiacal meaning to specific card meaning,
  not just a decorative label.
- Explicitly attributed to a named astrology-tarot tradition or school —
  like the existing Kabbalah correspondences, this kind of cross-tradition
  mapping is contested and school-specific, not universal, and should be
  presented that way.

**Layer support.** Would add a genuine astrological lens to Layer 4, if
and when Guardian wants one.

**Priority: LOW, deferred.** Astrology is an explicitly frozen domain per
`PROJECT_STATE.md` until Tarot and Numerology are further along. Listed
here for completeness, not as something to act on now.

## A note on high-leverage source types

Worth watching for while collecting sources, without letting it become a
requirement: a single source that combines art-historical or iconographic
*analysis* of Rider-Waite-Smith imagery (as opposed to either pure
description or pure interpretation) could plausibly serve #1, #2, and
part of #3 simultaneously, since that kind of analysis typically ties
specific symbols to specific traditional and esoteric meanings in one
pass. Not assumed to exist or required — just worth recognizing as
unusually high-leverage if something like it turns up.

## Non-goals of this document

No specific titles, authors, or publishers are named or implied above,
by design. This is a capability roadmap, not a shopping list — the actual
search for sources against it is a separate step.
